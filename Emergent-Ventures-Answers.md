## Read this before you type anything

Three things about this specific form that change what I gave you earlier:

**The proposal limit is 1,000 words, not 1,500.** Their instructions say "1000
words or fewer." My earlier pack said 1,500. Use 1,000.

**There is a question I did not know about** — "What is one mainstream or
'consensus' view that you absolutely agree with?" It is deliberately the reverse
of the usual contrarian question, and it is a real test of whether you think or
just perform. It needs a genuine answer.

**They screen for AI writing and reject on it.** Their own page says: "We will
test all submissions and reject those using AI for anything other than
proofreading." So the text below is *scaffolding with your facts in it* — not a
paste-ready submission. The four passages marked **[YOURS]** must be written by
you, from memory, in your own words. If you paste the rest verbatim it will read
smoothly and generic, which is exactly what they are filtering for.

A practical test: read it aloud. Any sentence you would not say to a friend,
rewrite.

## Section 3 — Proposal

### How do you describe your idea in a tweet?*

One sentence. No hashtag, no emoji, no "🚀". Aim for ~120 characters.

**Use this, or close to it:**

> A legal, dinar-priced way for Tunisian developers to buy frontier AI — when no
> international card and no local invoice exists.

**Why this works:** it names the customer, the country, the mechanism and the
obstacle in one line. Alternatives if you want a different tone:

> Tunisian developers cannot legally buy frontier AI. I built the gateway,
> billing and metering that fixes it.

> The only legal, locally-priced way for a developer in Tunisia to buy frontier

### Tell us about your proposal*

#### PART 1 — About you

*Their instruction: "Tell us your personal story, and how it relates to what you
wish to do. You don't need to impress us with stories of your educational
credentials, or awards."*

**[YOURS — write this yourself, 150–200 words. Do not use my words here.]**

What they want is one concrete scene, not a summary. Prompts to write from:

- What were you actually trying to build when you hit the wall?
- What did you try first? A friend's account? A reseller? What happened?
- What did it cost you — in money, in time, in work you could not do?
- Was there a specific moment you decided to build this instead of working around it?

The strongest version of this answer is specific and slightly uncomfortable.
"I could not afford it" is weak. "I spent three weeks building around a model I
could have used in an afternoon, because I had no way to pay for it from here" is
strong.

Avoid: your degree, your GPA, your internships, adjectives about your own
ambition.

#### PART 2 — The consensus question

*Their instruction: "What is one mainstream or 'consensus' view that you
absolutely agree with? (This is our version of a 'trick' question, reversing the
now-fashionable contrarian.)"*

**[YOURS — but here is how to think about it, 100–150 words.]**

This question is testing whether you hold views because you examined them or
because they are fashionable in your peer group. A contrarian answer here is a
failure — they have explicitly inverted the usual test.

Pick something you actually believe that is unfashionable *to disagree with* in
your own circles. For a developer in Tunisia, honest candidates:

- That building on top of someone else's model is real work, not a lesser form
  of it — the consensus in some founder circles is that only proprietary models
  count, and you disagree.
- That charging customers money early is more honest than raising money and
  calling it validation.
- That most developers do not need the most powerful model, they need reliable
  access to a good one — and the industry's obsession with the frontier is
  partly a distraction.
- That regulation and compliance, usually treated as friction, are what make a
  market possible at all.

Pick the one you can defend in two sentences with a specific example. Do not
pick the one that sounds cleverest.

#### PART 3 — About your idea

*Their instruction: convince us this is a great idea worth doing. A plan is not
required; bullet points are fine. Cover: problem, solution, novel aspects,
timing, budget outline, implementation plan, expected impact. Also tell us how
long you have worked on it, whether you have a prototype, and name any
cofounders.*

**[Use this structure and these facts. Rewrite the sentences in your own voice.]**

**The problem.** Tunisia has roughly 30,000 developers. Almost none can legally
buy a frontier AI subscription. There is no internationally accepted payment
card, no invoice in dinars, and no licensed local reseller. So the market filled
with informal ones: people reselling access through chat groups, taking payment
by transfer, with no recourse if the account disappears. The alternative is
borrowing a colleague's login. Either way, a developer here works with a toolset
years behind a developer in Paris, and the gap compounds.

**The solution.** Zaino is a metered subscription gateway. A developer pays in
dinars through D17 — the La Poste Tunisienne wallet almost everyone already has
— and gets access to the same models their peers abroad use, with a monthly
token allowance, a dashboard, and an API key that works with standard tooling.
The models are not mine. The way in is.

**What is actually new.** Not inference. The hard part in this market is the
layer around it: metering precisely enough that a customer cannot consume more
than they paid for, enforcing that quota on every single request and failing
closed, billing in a currency with no card rail, and doing all of it inside the
rules. That is what I built, and it is why nobody here has done it. The
compliance wrapper is the product, not a constraint on it.

**Timing.** Two things changed recently. Frontier model providers now offer
reseller and partner arrangements that did not exist two years ago. And
Tunisia's Startup Act gives labelled companies a corporate tax exemption, a
foreign-currency account, and a state founder's grant — a legal foundation that
also did not exist before 2018. Both windows are open at once.

**How long, and what exists.** [X months] of work. This is not a proposal: the
system runs today. A quota engine that enforces a hard monthly limit per
subscription on every request; two payment rails, one live and one built and in
test mode; a trilingual dashboard in French, Arabic and English; a desktop
client with browser-based device login so users never handle a raw key; 641
automated tests passing and a clean type-check across 40 database migrations.

**Budget outline.** I am asking for $1,000.

- Incorporating the company (SUARL, doing the filing myself): 1,885 DT ≈ $608
- Apple Developer Program, so the desktop client can ship: $99
- Startup Label application fee: 100 DT ≈ $32
- Bank, notary and courier costs: ~573 DT ≈ $185

That totals $924. I am asking for $1,000, and the difference covers exchange
and bank charges on receiving the grant.

**Implementation plan.** Incorporate within four weeks of funding. Apply for the
Startup Label immediately after — 100 DT, decided within 30 days. Within one
month of the label, file for the Bourse Startup, a state grant of 1,000 DT net
per month for twelve months for a non-salaried founder. That is 12,000 DT, and
it is why $1,000 here is not a subsidy: it is the key that unlocks twelve times
its value in non-dilutive public funding. Then open paid subscriptions on the
D17 rail.

**Expected impact.** Directly: a legal way for Tunisian developers to buy the
tools of their trade, priced in the currency they earn. Indirectly: a company
that pays tax here rather than money leaving the country through informal
channels, and a template other developers in the region can copy — the same
obstacle exists in Algeria, Morocco and Egypt.

**Cofounders.** None. Sole founder. [If you have one, name them here and say
clearly who the point of contact is.]
